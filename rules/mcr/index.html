CH. 3

Special Proceedings and Actions

MCR 3.208
Writ of Quo Warranto — Special Provisions
/rules/mcr/#mcr-3-208

(A)A petition for a writ of quo warranto shall be filed in the supreme court and may be initiated by the attorney general or, upon leave of the court, by a private individual upon a showing of special interest as provided in subsection (B).

(B)Private Individual — Leave to File.

(1)Special Interest Defined. For purposes of this rule, a private individual has a "special interest" in the office or franchise at issue if the individual demonstrates an interest in the subject matter distinct from that of the public generally, including but not limited to:

(a)the individual is an elector eligible to vote for the office at issue;

(b)the individual is a rival claimant to the office or franchise at issue; or

(c)the individual demonstrates a direct pecuniary or legal interest that would be affected by the respondent's continued exercise of the office or franchise.

(2)Application for Leave. A private individual seeking leave to file a petition under this rule shall file a written application for leave, supported by affidavit, that:

(a)identifies the office or franchise at issue and the basis on which the respondent is alleged to unlawfully hold or exercise it;

(b)states the facts establishing the applicant's special interest under subsection (B)(1); and

(c)attaches the proposed petition for the writ of quo warranto.

(3)Standard for Leave. The supreme court shall grant leave to file where the applicant demonstrates a special interest under subsection (B)(1) and the proposed petition, on its face, states a basis on which relief could be granted.

(4)Denial. An order denying leave to file shall briefly state the reason for denial and is not subject to further review.

(C)The petition shall identify the public office or franchise at issue and the basis upon which the petitioner contends the respondent unlawfully holds or exercises that office or franchise.

(D)The respondent shall have the right to answer the petition, present evidence, and be represented by counsel.

Jul 1, 2026
Added subsection (B), defining "special interest" and establishing the application and standard for leave to file by a private individual. Prior text lacked any definition or procedure for this term.

MCR 3.908
Appeal
/rules/mcr/#mcr-3-908

(A)Claim of Appeal. A petitioner aggrieved by a denial of an application to set aside a conviction or arrest may file a claim of appeal as of right within 14 days after entry of the order of denial, provided one or more of the following applies:

(1)the petitioner identifies a palpable error by which the court and the parties were misled, and shows that correcting the error would require a different disposition of the application;

(2)the petitioner's application was reviewed on the papers, provided the petitioner did not waive the hearing on the application; or

(3)the petitioner's application was reviewed by a magistrate.

(B)Reviewing Officer. A claim of appeal filed under this rule shall be reviewed as follows:

(1)if the application was reviewed by a magistrate, a judge shall review the claim of appeal;

(2)if the application was reviewed by a judge, the chief judge shall review the claim of appeal.

(C)Form. The State Court Administrative Office shall maintain a form for a claim of appeal filed under this rule.

(D)Hearing.

(1)A claim of appeal filed under this rule shall by default entitle the petitioner to a hearing, subject to payment of the applicable hearing fee.

(2)The petitioner may waive the hearing using the form described in subsection (C).

(3)In lieu of payment, the petitioner may file a fee waiver request under MCR 7.403.

(4)Absent a waiver under subsection (D)(2), a hearing shall be scheduled by default.

(E)Brief. A claim of appeal filed under this rule shall not require an accompanying brief, except that a petitioner proceeding under subsection (A)(1) shall submit a brief in support of the claimed palpable error. A petitioner not otherwise required to submit a brief may nonetheless do so.

(F)Application for Leave to Appeal. A petitioner who does not qualify to file a claim of appeal under subsection (A), or who fails to file a claim of appeal within the time provided in subsection (A), may file an Application for Leave to Appeal Order Denying Application to Set Aside Conviction or Arrest within 60 days after entry of the order of denial.

(1)This application is not a form maintained by the State Court Administrative Office and shall consist of a brief prepared by the petitioner.

(2)The petitioner may raise any basis for the application, provided the basis has merit and is not frivolous.

(3)The court to which the application is directed shall grant leave to appeal upon a determination that the appeal presents a substantial question warranting review.

(G)Finality. A decision denying a claim of appeal under subsection (A) or an application for leave to appeal under subsection (F) is final and is not subject to further review, except that the petitioner may seek review of that denial by filing an application for leave to appeal with the supreme court within 21 days after entry of the order denying the claim of appeal or application for leave.

Jul 1, 2026
Added subsection (G), establishing that a denial under (A) or (F) is final subject to a 21-day window to seek supreme court review.
Mar 15, 2026
Replaced the prior single application-for-leave track (21-day deadline, abuse-of-discretion review) with the two-track structure in subsections (A)–(F): a 14-day claim of appeal as of right on enumerated grounds, and a 60-day discretionary application for leave for all other cases.

CH. 5

District Court

MCR 5.101
Scope and Applicability
/rules/mcr/#mcr-5-101

(A)This chapter governs procedure and administration in the district courts of this state, including case assignment, scheduling, docket management, the authority of magistrate judges, court records, court decorum, and the rights of pro se litigants.

(B)Subject-Matter Jurisdiction. The district courts of this state are courts of general trial jurisdiction and have subject-matter jurisdiction over all civil and criminal matters not committed exclusively to the supreme court, including:

(1)all civil actions at law and in equity, regardless of the amount in controversy;

(2)small claims actions, landlord-tenant actions, and summary proceedings to recover possession of premises;

(3)all criminal matters, including felonies, misdemeanors, ordinance violations, and civil infractions, together with related pretrial proceedings such as arraignment, bail, and preliminary examination;

(4)probate, family, and juvenile matters as provided by statute; and

(5)any other matter over which subject-matter jurisdiction is conferred on the district courts by statute.

(C)Where this chapter does not address a procedural matter arising in a district court proceeding, the applicable rules of Chapters 2, 3, or 6 shall govern depending on the nature of the proceeding.

(D)These rules shall be construed to promote the efficient, fair, and accessible administration of justice in the district courts of this state.

Jul 1, 2026
Added subsection (B), stating the district court's subject-matter jurisdiction as a court of general trial jurisdiction; renumbered former (B) and (C) as (C) and (D).

Appeals

MCR 7.901
Transfer of Cases Between Judges
/rules/mcr/#mcr-7-901

(A)The chief judge may transfer a case from one judge or magistrate judge to another within the same district court upon any of the following grounds:

(1)Recusal or disqualification of the assigned judge;

(2)Extended absence or incapacity of the assigned judge;

(3)Undue concentration of caseload on a particular judge; or

(4)Any other administrative reason that serves the interests of justice and the efficient operation of the court.

(B)A transfer under this rule shall be made by written order of the chief judge entered into the record and served on all parties through the Notice of Electronic Filing system.

(C)Upon transfer, the receiving judge shall have full authority over the matter and shall not be required to repeat any proceedings already completed unless the receiving judge determines repetition is necessary for a fair adjudication.

(D)A party who believes a transfer has been made improperly may file a motion objecting to the transfer within 7 days of receiving notice of the transfer. The chief judge shall rule on the motion within 10 days of filing.

MCR 7.902
Designation of a Justice Upon Unavailability of All Judges of a District Court
/rules/mcr/#mcr-7-902

(A)Grounds. Where all judges and magistrate judges of a district court are simultaneously unavailable to hear a matter — whether by recusal, disqualification, absence, incapacity, or vacancy — the chief judge, or any party if there is no chief judge able to act, shall notify the Supreme Court of the unavailability.

(B)Designation. Upon receiving notice under subsection (A), the Chief Justice shall designate an associate justice of the Supreme Court to sit temporarily as a judge of the district court for purposes of hearing and deciding the matter, or such matters, as necessitated by the unavailability.

(C)Scope of Designation. A designation under this rule:

(1)shall specify whether it extends to a single case, a class of matters, or all matters before the district court until further order;

(2)may be limited in duration or terminated by subsequent order of the Chief Justice; and

(3)confers on the designated justice all the authority of a judge of the district court with respect to the matter or matters designated, including the authority to conduct trials, enter judgments, and rule on post-judgment motions.

(D)Notice to Parties. An order of designation shall be entered into the record of the affected case or cases and served on all parties through the Notice of Electronic Filing system.

(E)Return of Matter. Upon the resolution of the unavailability described in subsection (A) — including the appointment or return of a district court judge, or the resolution of a recusal — the designated justice shall, unless the interests of justice require otherwise, return any pending matters to the district court for further proceedings, and the designation shall terminate as to those matters.

(F)No Repetition Required. A designated justice is not required to repeat any proceedings already completed before the district court became unavailable, unless the designated justice determines repetition is necessary for a fair adjudication.

Jul 1, 2026
New rule adopted, addressing the gap in MCR 7.901 for cases where every judge of a district court is simultaneously unavailable, by providing for temporary designation of a supreme court justice.

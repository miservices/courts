Judicial Tenure Commission

The Commission receives and investigates complaints about the conduct of judges and other judicial officers.

About the Commission

The Judicial Tenure Commission was established by Administrative Order No. 2 to receive, investigate, and act on allegations about the conduct of judicial officers. It exists so that people have a place to raise concerns about how a judge or magistrate behaves, and so those concerns are looked into fairly.

The Commission is independent of the courts it reviews. It has three commissioners, and one of them serves as chairperson. No commissioner may be a former or current justice, or a current judge or magistrate.

What the Commission does

  • Receives complaints about judicial conduct.
  • Investigates the allegations using its own procedures.
  • Recommends action to the Supreme Court when it finds it appropriate, such as discipline, suspension, or removal of a judicial officer.
  • Reports to the Court on its proceedings and recommendations when the Court requires.

The Commission recommends. The Supreme Court decides. Any discipline, suspension, or removal is the Supreme Court's decision, based on the Commission's recommendation.

What the Commission can't do

The Commission looks at a judicial officer's conduct. It is not a way to appeal a ruling. If you disagree with how a judge decided your case, the remedy is an appeal, not a complaint. See mandatory appeals.

  • It can't change, reverse, or reopen the outcome of a case.
  • It can't give legal advice or represent you.
  • It can't decide a judge's punishment by itself. It can only recommend to the Supreme Court.

How a complaint works

  1. You file a complaintDescribe what the judicial officer did, when it happened, and which case it relates to.
  2. The Commission reviews itThe commissioners look at whether the allegation concerns judicial conduct and investigate under the Commission's procedures.
  3. The Commission decides what to recommendIf action is warranted, it recommends discipline, suspension, removal, or another appropriate step to the Supreme Court.
  4. The Supreme Court actsThe Court receives the Commission's report and takes final action.

What to include in a complaint

  • The name of the judge or magistrate and the court they serve in
  • The case number, if it relates to a case
  • The date and a clear description of what happened
  • Any documents or witnesses that support your complaint
